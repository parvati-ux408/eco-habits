The problem

In Mira-Bhayandar, many college students may lack awareness of sustainable consumption, leading to excess waste and irresponsible resource use. There is no simple, low-cost tool for them to measure their own habits or understand what stops them from choosing eco-friendly options.

Why current approaches fall short

Posters and campaigns, college seminars, paper surveys and cleanliness drives all share the same gaps: no local data, low student engagement, no personal feedback, and slow manual analysis.

A lightweight website that needs no server or database and works on any phone or laptop.

Key findings: 69% have heard the term, but only 44% rate their understanding 4 or 5. Availability, awareness and cost are the biggest barriers, and lower prices is the top motivator. Only 11 of 36 find sustainable products affordable, and 56% think their college can help. Students suggested campus swap meets and reuse hubs, water refill stations, clearly labelled recycling bins, and discounts for reusable mugs.

Conclusion and future scope

A simple HTML/CSS and Google Forms/Sheets platform can measure habits at very low cost, and the results can guide awareness efforts that support SDGs 4, 8 and 10.
